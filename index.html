About

Applied ML, end to end

I work across the whole of an applied ML project: preparing data, training or orchestrating a model, serving it behind a small API, and building an interface someone else can actually use.

The step I care about most is evaluation — scoring the saved model rather than trusting a notebook printout, saying where every number comes from, and writing down what the system gets wrong. My current interests are graph learning, agentic LLM workflows and retrieval-augmented generation.

How these projects were built Each of the five projects started from a public tutorial. Each was then extended with a research-style interface, a re-evaluation of the shipped model or pipeline, deployment, and a written account of its limitations. Every case study lists what was added. The extensions were developed with the help of AI coding assistants; the evaluations and results were run and checked on the real systems.

Method

How I evaluate

A number is only useful if a reader can tell where it came from and what it leaves out.

01

Score the shipped artifact

Metrics are recomputed from the saved model files on the original held-out split, not copied from a single notebook run.

02

Label the provenance

Every figure is marked as re-evaluated, measured in a live run, or copied from notebook output.

03

Compare against something

A baseline or an ablation where one exists — a random forest without graph structure, the GCN without citation edges, logistic regression for credit risk.

04

Write down the failures

Data leakage, a checkpointing bug, a threshold chosen on the test set, and a speaker-attribution error are documented, including the ones not yet fixed.
